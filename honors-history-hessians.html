Project overview

About this research

This honors history paper examines the Hessians through journals, travel accounts, and memoirs from the American Revolution and its aftermath. It compares five primary-source works to explore scientific observation, religious participation, and relationships with American communities. Through close reading and source criticism, the paper argues that the familiar mercenary image leaves out important parts of Hessian life. It also considers how each writer’s background shapes the record and identifies regional comparisons as a next step for research.

The question

What do Hessian journals and memoirs reveal about lives and contributions beyond military service?

The approach

How I explored it

Close reading of five cited primary-source works, source criticism, comparison across authors, and thematic grouping within a group-history approach.

What the paper discusses

Ideas & findings

  • The paper argues that a mercenary stereotype overlooks scientific observation, religious participation, craftsmanship, and community connections.
  • It compares authors with different social positions and considers how those perspectives shape the surviving record.
  • It proposes wider regional comparisons using marriage, property, court, and military records as future research.

Scope & next steps

The conclusions are historical interpretations developed from selected journals and memoirs. The paper identifies wider regional comparisons and additional records as useful next steps.
